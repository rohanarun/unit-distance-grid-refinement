\section{Unit distances: a smaller final generic grid}
\label{sec:smaller-grid}
The algebra section of OpenAI's
\href{https://github.com/openai/math/tree/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/preprints/A-power-saving-for-planar-unit-distances-September-23-2026}{unit-distance manuscript}
selects $N=20$ left points and $j=5$ right points. Its final sign-change
argument only needs four anchors in the rank-two case and three in the
common-line case. A normalization also reduces the coefficient-field
cost from $3j$ to $3j-4$.

Retain the original differential system, with $J(x,y)=(-y,x)$:
\[
 2(p_i-r_\ell)\cdot(H_i-U_\ell)
   =R_{i\ell}(A_i-A'_\ell),\qquad A_i-A'_\ell\ne0.
\]
Let $v=r_2-r_1$. The anchors are distinct real points at finite indices,
so $v\cdot v\ne0$ in the ultraproduct field. Define
\[
 \omega=\frac{(U_2-U_1)\cdot Jv}{v\cdot v},
\]
\[
 \widetilde H_i=H_i-U_1-\omega J(p_i-r_1),\qquad
 \widetilde U_\ell=U_\ell-U_1-\omega J(r_\ell-r_1),
\]
\[
 \widetilde A_i=A_i-A'_1,\qquad
 \widetilde A'_\ell=A'_\ell-A'_1.
\]
Because $x\cdot Jx=0$, these replacements preserve the differential
system and its nonzero scalar differences. They need not themselves
be values of a new derivation; the later linear-algebra argument only
needs the preserved equations. They satisfy
\[
 \widetilde U_1=0,\quad \widetilde A'_1=0,\quad
 \widetilde U_2=\tau v
\]
for one scalar $\tau$. Thus, after all anchor positions have been adjoined,
the normalized right-hand data require at most
\[
 1+2(j-2)+(j-1)=3j-4
\]
additional field generators.

\begin{proposition}[Reduction of the final algebraic witness]
Conditional on the source's generic-grid and private-valuation lemmas,
the final algebraic contradiction can be run with a $9$-by-$4$ grid
instead of a $20$-by-$5$ grid.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the source's generic-grid lemma with $N=9,j=4$. Let $K$ be the
algebraic closure, inside the source's algebraically closed ambient field,
of the field generated by $B_0$, the four anchor positions, and the
normalized data just described. In particular this does not adjoin the
entire negligible-height field. The data cost at most eight generators.
If the left locus has dimension $d_1\in\{1,2\}$, the left points jointly
retain transcendence degree at least $9d_1-8$ over $K$.
If none were individually generic, subadditivity would give at most
$9(d_1-1)=9d_1-9$, a contradiction. A generic left point therefore
survives over $K$.

The source constructs private odd valuations directly after enlargement
of the coefficient field using the original independence of positions.
That proof applies over this $K$ as well. Hence the four radicands have
independent square classes in $K(p)$. In the rank-two case the first
three equations determine
$\widetilde H_x,\widetilde H_y,\widetilde A$ in the field generated
by the first three radicals. The automorphism changing only the fourth
radical then forces $\widetilde A=\widetilde A'_4$, a contradiction.
In the common-line case, the first two equations determine the scalar
velocity along the line and $\widetilde A$; the third radical gives the
same contradiction. These are the source's two Cramer's-rule arguments
with normalized coefficients. Four anchors suffice in either case.
\end{proof}
This reduces the selected grid from $100$ edges to $36$. It does not
provide an explicit power saving $\delta$ for the unit-distance bound:
the generic-grid extraction and negligible-height passage remain
asymptotic, and no quantitative replacement is established here.
