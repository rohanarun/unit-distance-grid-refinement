\section{The symmetry group of the differential system}
\label{sec:symmetries}
Retain the original differential system, with $J(x,y)=(-y,x)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:system}
 2(p_i-r_\ell)\cdot(H_i-U_\ell)
   =R_{i\ell}(A_i-A'_\ell),\qquad A_i-A'_\ell\ne0 .
\end{equation}
Here $H_i=\partial p_i$, $U_\ell=\partial r_\ell$,
$A_i=\partial g_i/g_i$ and $A'_\ell=\partial h_\ell/h_\ell$ for the
source's $k_0$-derivation $\partial$ and its normalized ratios
$g_i,h_\ell$. Since $h_1=\lambda_{11}/\lambda_{11}=1$, one has
$A'_1=0$ automatically.

For $T\in\mathcal U^2$ and $\omega,c\in\mathcal U$ with $c\ne0$, the
replacement
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge}
 H_i\mapsto c\bigl(H_i-T-\omega Jp_i\bigr),\quad
 U_\ell\mapsto c\bigl(U_\ell-T-\omega Jr_\ell\bigr),\quad
 A_i\mapsto cA_i,\quad A'_\ell\mapsto cA'_\ell
\end{equation}
preserves \eqref{eq:system}, including the nonvanishing of
$A_i-A'_\ell$. The isometric part preserves each left side because
$x\cdot Jx=0$, and the common factor $c$ multiplies both sides. The
scaled data are the values of the derivation $c\,\partial$; the shifted
data need not be values of a new derivation, and none is needed: the
later linear algebra uses only the preserved equations.

\paragraph{Normalization.}
Let $v=r_2-r_1$. The anchors are distinct real points at finite indices,
so $v\cdot v\ne0$ in the ultraproduct field. Apply \eqref{eq:gauge} with
$T=U_1$, $c=1$ and
\[
 \omega=\frac{(U_2-U_1)\cdot Jv}{v\cdot v}.
\]
The new data satisfy $\widetilde U_1=0$ and
$\widetilde U_2\cdot Jv=0$, hence $\widetilde U_2=\tau v$ with
\[
 \tau=\frac{(U_2-U_1)\cdot v}{v\cdot v}\in F .
\]
If $\tau\ne0$, apply \eqref{eq:gauge} again with $T=0$, $\omega=0$,
$c=1/\tau$. In both cases the final normalized data satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized}
 \widetilde U_1=0,\qquad
 \widetilde U_2\in\{0,v\},\qquad
 \widetilde A'_1=0 .
\end{equation}
Since $v\in B_0(r_1,r_2)^2$, the normalized right-hand data require,
after all anchor positions have been adjoined, at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cost}
 2(j-2)+(j-1)=3j-5
\end{equation}
additional field generators: two for each of
$\widetilde U_3,\ldots,\widetilde U_j$ and one for each of
$\widetilde A'_2,\ldots,\widetilde A'_j$.

\begin{proposition}[Reduction of the final algebraic witness]
\label{prop:general}
Conditional on the source's generic-grid and private-valuation lemmas,
the final algebraic contradiction can be run with an $8$-by-$4$ grid
instead of a $20$-by-$5$ grid.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the source's generic-grid lemma with $N=8$, $j=4$. Let $K$ be the
algebraic closure, inside the source's algebraically closed ambient
field, of the field generated by $B_0$, the four anchor positions, and
the normalized data \eqref{eq:normalized}. This does not adjoin the
negligible-height field. By \eqref{eq:cost} the data cost at most seven
generators. If the left locus has dimension $d_1\in\{1,2\}$, the left
points jointly retain transcendence degree at least $8d_1-7$ over $K$.
If none were individually generic, subadditivity would give at most
$8(d_1-1)=8d_1-8$, a contradiction. A generic left point $p$ therefore
survives over $K$; write $M=K(p)$.

The source constructs private odd valuations directly after enlargement
of the coefficient field, using only the original joint genericity of
the positions over $B_0$. That proof applies over this $K$. Hence the
four radicands have independent square classes in $M$, and the Kummer
statement of the source holds in $M(R_1,\ldots,R_4)$.

If the rows $p-r_1,p-r_2,p-r_3$ have rank two, the first three
equations of \eqref{eq:system}, with the normalized data on the right,
have coefficients and right sides in $M(R_1,R_2,R_3)$ and a determinant
that is a nonzero $M$-linear combination of $R_1,R_2,R_3$; Cramer's rule
places $\widetilde H_x,\widetilde H_y,\widetilde A$ in that field. The
automorphism changing only the sign of $R_4$ fixes these unknowns.
Applied to the fourth equation and subtracted, it gives
$2R_4(\widetilde A-\widetilde A'_4)=0$, so
$\widetilde A=\widetilde A'_4$, contradicting \eqref{eq:system}.
The rank condition fails only when both loci are the same line, which
is handled with fewer points in Section~\ref{sec:common-line}; the
$8$-by-$4$ grid contains the $4$-by-$3$ sub-grid used there.
\end{proof}

Eight is the least value this count allows: with $N=7$ the two sides of
$7d_1-7>7(d_1-1)$ are equal.

\section{Collinear anchors need six left points}
\label{sec:collinear}
The proposition's hypotheses are symmetric under exchanging the two
vertex sets. Suppose that one of the two loci is a line, and name the
sides so that the anchor locus $V_2$ is that line. Write
\[
 r_\ell=r_1+\sigma_\ell v,\qquad
 \sigma_\ell=\frac{(r_\ell-r_1)\cdot v}{v\cdot v}\in F,\qquad
 \sigma_1=0,\ \sigma_2=1 .
\]
The derivation satisfies the Leibniz rule, so
\[
 U_\ell-U_1=\partial(\sigma_\ell v)
   =\sigma_\ell\,\partial v+(\partial\sigma_\ell)\,v .
\]
With $\omega$ as in Section~\ref{sec:symmetries}, the vector
$\partial v-\omega Jv$ is orthogonal to $Jv$, hence equals
$\kappa v$ with $\kappa=(\partial v\cdot v)/(v\cdot v)$, because
$v\cdot Jv=0$ and $v\cdot v\ne0$. Therefore every normalized anchor
velocity is parallel to the line:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parallel}
 \widetilde U_\ell=U_\ell-U_1-\omega J(r_\ell-r_1)
   =\mu_\ell v,\qquad
 \mu_\ell=\sigma_\ell\kappa+\partial\sigma_\ell,\qquad
 \mu_1=0,\ \mu_2=\kappa=\tau .
\end{equation}
After the scaling step, $\mu_2\in\{0,1\}$. The normalized data are then
the scalars $\mu_3,\ldots,\mu_j$ and
$\widetilde A'_2,\ldots,\widetilde A'_j$, so they cost at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collinear-cost}
 (j-2)+(j-1)=2j-3
\end{equation}
generators over $B_0(r_1,\ldots,r_j)$.

\begin{proposition}[One locus is a line]
\label{prop:collinear}
Conditional on the same source lemmas, if either locus is a line the
final contradiction can be run with a $6$-by-$4$ grid whose four
anchors lie on that line.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $N=6$, $j=4$ with the anchors on the line. By
\eqref{eq:collinear-cost} the data cost at most five generators, and
$6d_1-5>6(d_1-1)$ leaves a generic left point $p$ over $K$. If
$V_1=V_2$, Section~\ref{sec:common-line} applies to a sub-grid.
Otherwise $p$ is generic on the irreducible locus $V_1\ne V_2$, so it
does not lie on the anchor line, and the rows $p-r_1,p-r_2,p-r_3$ have
rank two. The right sides of \eqref{eq:system} are
$2\mu_\ell(p-r_\ell)\cdot v-R_\ell\widetilde A'_\ell\in M(R_\ell)$, and
the Cramer and sign-change steps of
Proposition~\ref{prop:general} apply verbatim.
\end{proof}

\section{The common-line branch needs only twelve selected edges}
\label{sec:common-line}
Suppose both loci are the same line. Then $p-r_\ell=s_\ell v$ with
$s_\ell\in M^\times$, only $h=v\cdot\widetilde H$ and $\widetilde A$
enter \eqref{eq:system}, and the right sides are
$2s_\ell\mu_\ell(v\cdot v)-R_\ell\widetilde A'_\ell$. Three anchors
suffice: the first two equations determine $h$ and $\widetilde A$, and
the third radical supplies the sign change. For $j=3$ the collinear
cost \eqref{eq:collinear-cost} is three generators,
$\mu_3,\widetilde A'_2,\widetilde A'_3$. Select $N=4$ left points. In
this case $d_1=1$, and
\[
 4\cdot1-3>4\cdot0 ,
\]
so a left point survives as generic over $K$. The first two equations
have coefficient matrix
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}2s_1&-R_1\\2s_2&-R_2\end{pmatrix}
\]
with determinant $2(s_2R_1-s_1R_2)$, nonzero by the independent
single-radical basis and $s_1,s_2\ne0$. Cramer's rule places $h$ and
$\widetilde A$ in the field of the first two radicals. Flipping only
$R_3$ in the third equation and subtracting forces
$\widetilde A=\widetilde A'_3$, contrary to the preserved nonzero
scalar difference. Thus a $4$-by-$3$ witness, or twelve edges, suffices
for this branch. Three left points would give $3\cdot1-3=0$ and prove
nothing.

\section{Thirty edges without a surviving point}
\label{sec:joint}
The arguments so far adjoin the anchor data to the coefficient field
and then look for one left point that stays generic. This section
instead treats the velocity data of all grid vertices as unknowns of a
single homogeneous linear system and shows that the system has no
solution with $A_i\ne A'_\ell$. The count is then $Nj$ equations
against $3(N+j)$ unknowns with a four-dimensional trivial kernel, so
$Nj\ge3(N+j)-4$ is the natural requirement, and $N=6$, $j=5$ is the
smallest grid meeting it.

\subsection{Setup}
Take $N=6$ and $j=5$ in the source's generic-grid lemma. Write
$E=\{(i,\ell)\}$ for the thirty grid edges, $R_e=R_{i\ell}$,
$D_e=L_\ell(p_i)\bigl(L_\ell(p_i)-4\bigr)=R_e^2$. Let $K'$ be the
algebraic closure in $\mathcal U$ of $B_0(r_1,\ldots,r_5)$, let
$M''=K'(p_1,\ldots,p_6)$, and let
$\mathcal M''=M''(R_e:e\in E)\subset\mathcal U$. No velocity data are
adjoined anywhere.

\begin{lemma}[Generic evaluation on the product]
\label{lem:product}
A polynomial with coefficients in $B_0$ in the coordinates of the
grid points that vanishes at the grid vanishes on all of
$V_1^6\times V_2^5$ over $\mathcal U$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
View the polynomial as a polynomial in the coordinates of one grid
point with coefficients in the field generated by the others. That
point is generic on its locus over this field, by the joint
transcendence count of the generic-grid lemma, so the source's
evaluation argument shows the polynomial vanishes at every
$\mathcal U$-point of that locus when the other points are held at
their grid values. Repeating for each point in turn, with the
remaining sub-grid still jointly generic, gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[All thirty radicands are independent]
\label{lem:thirty}
The classes of $D_e$, $e\in E$, are independent in
$(M'')^\times/((M'')^\times)^2$. Consequently
$[\mathcal M'':M'']=2^{30}$, and the products
$\prod_{e\in S}R_e$, $S\subseteq E$, form an $M''$-basis of
$\mathcal M''$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $i$ and let $K_i$ be the algebraic closure of
$K'(p_{i'}:i'\ne i)$. By the joint transcendence count, $p_i$ is
generic on $V_1$ over $K_i$. The source's private-valuation lemma,
applied with coefficient field $K_i$, gives for each $\ell$ a
valuation $v_{i\ell}$ of $K_i(p_i)\supseteq M''$ with
$v_{i\ell}(D_{i\ell})=1$ and $v_{i\ell}(D_{im})=0$ for $m\ne\ell$;
its noncoincidence inputs are statements about anchors proved from
joint genericity over $B_0$ and are unaffected by the larger
coefficient field, as the source itself notes. For $i'\ne i$ the
element $D_{i'\ell'}$ lies in $K_i^\times$ and has valuation zero. So
every nonempty product of distinct $D_e$ has odd valuation at some
$v_{i\ell}$ and is not a square in $M''$. The degree and basis
statements are the quadratic case of Kummer theory, as in the source.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The homogeneous system}
Order the unknowns as
$(H_1,\ldots,H_6,U_1,\ldots,U_5,A_1,\ldots,A_6,A'_1,\ldots,A'_5)
\in\mathcal U^{33}$. For $e=(i,\ell)$ the source's differential
equation \eqref{eq:system} reads $\rho_e\cdot x=0$ with
\[
 \rho_e=a_e+R_e\,b_e ,
\]
where $a_e\in(M'')^{33}$ is supported on the velocity coordinates,
with entries $2(p_i-r_\ell)$ at $H_i$ and $-2(p_i-r_\ell)$ at
$U_\ell$, and $b_e$ is supported on the scalar coordinates, with
$-1$ at $A_i$ and $+1$ at $A'_\ell$. Let $\mathbf R$ be the
$30\times33$ matrix with rows $\rho_e$. The actual data
$x^*=(H_i,U_\ell,A_i,A'_\ell)\in F^{33}$ satisfy
$\mathbf Rx^*=0$ and $A_i-A'_\ell\ne0$ for every edge.

Let $\operatorname{Rig}$ be the $30\times22$ matrix with rows the
velocity parts $a_e$: up to the factor two it is the rigidity matrix
of the complete bipartite framework $K_{6,5}$ on the grid points. Its
kernel, the space of infinitesimal motions, contains the three
dimensional trivial motions $H_i=T+\omega Jp_i$,
$U_\ell=T+\omega Jr_\ell$. Set
\[
 Z=\{(y_{\mathrm{vel}},\beta\mathbf 1):
   \operatorname{Rig}\,y_{\mathrm{vel}}=0,\ \beta\in\mathcal M''\}
 \subseteq\ker\mathbf R ,
\]
the kernel vectors whose scalar part is constant. Then
$\dim_{\mathcal M''}Z=23-\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Rig}$, and
$\operatorname{rank}\mathbf R\le10+\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Rig}$.

\begin{lemma}[Rank from a spanning tree]
\label{lem:tree}
Let $T\subseteq E$ be a spanning tree of $K_{6,5}$. Then
\[
 \operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal M''}\mathbf R
 \ \ge\ 10+\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Rig}(E\setminus T),
\]
where $\operatorname{Rig}(E\setminus T)$ is the rigidity matrix with
the tree rows deleted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The vectors $b_e$, $e\in T$, are the signed incidence vectors of a
tree and are linearly independent; choose ten scalar columns on which
their determinant is nonzero. Choose a set $C\subseteq E\setminus T$
of $\rho'=\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Rig}(E\setminus T)$ edges
with independent rows $a_e$ and $\rho'$ velocity columns on which
their determinant is nonzero. Consider the minor of $\mathbf R$ on
the rows $T\cup C$ and these $10+\rho'$ columns. Each $R_e$ occurs in
exactly one row, so the minor is multilinear in the $R_e$:
\[
 \sum_{S\subseteq T\cup C}\Bigl(\prod_{e\in S}R_e\Bigr)c_S,
 \qquad c_S\in M'' ,
\]
where $c_S$ is the determinant with rows $b_e$ for $e\in S$ and
$a_e$ for $e\notin S$. For $S=T$ the rows $b_e$ live on the scalar
columns and the rows $a_e$ on the velocity columns, so $c_T$ is, up
to sign, the product of the two chosen nonzero determinants. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:thirty} the monomials are linearly independent over
$M''$, so the minor is nonzero.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Thirty edges when neither locus is a line]
\label{prop:joint}
Suppose neither $V_1$ nor $V_2$ is a line. Then, conditional on the
source's generic-grid and private-valuation lemmas, the final
contradiction can be run with a $6$-by-$5$ grid.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid} below there is a spanning tree $T$ with
$\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Rig}(E\setminus T)
=\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Rig}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:tree} and the
upper bound give
$\operatorname{rank}\mathbf R=10+\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Rig}$,
so $\ker_{\mathcal M''}\mathbf R=Z$ by dimension. Since rank is
unchanged under extension of scalars,
$\ker_{\mathcal U}\mathbf R=Z\otimes\mathcal U$, every element of
which has constant scalar part. The data $x^*$ lie in this kernel, so
$A_1=A'_1$, contradicting \eqref{eq:system}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The rigidity input}
Fix the spanning tree
\[
 T=\{p_1r_4,\ p_2r_4,\ p_3r_5,\ p_4r_5,\ p_5r_1,\ p_5r_2,\ p_6r_3,\
      p_6r_4,\ p_6r_5,\ p_5r_5\}.
\]
Then $E\setminus T$ consists of the complete bipartite core
$\{p_1,\ldots,p_4\}\times\{r_1,r_2,r_3\}$ together with the eight
edges $r_5p_1,r_5p_2$; $r_4p_3,r_4p_4$; $p_6r_1,p_6r_2$;
$p_5r_3,p_5r_4$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:rigid}
If neither locus is a line, then
$\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Rig}(E\setminus T)
=\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Rig}$ at the generic grid.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Attaching a new vertex to two existing vertices raises the rank of a
rigidity matrix by exactly two unless the three points are collinear:
the two new rows restricted to the two new columns form the matrix
with rows $v-u_1$ and $v-u_2$. Each of the four attachments in
$E\setminus T$ joins a generic point of one locus to two generic
points of the other; collinearity would, by Lemma~\ref{lem:product},
place a locus inside a line, which is excluded. Hence
\[
 \operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Rig}(E\setminus T)
 =\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Rig}(\text{core})+8 .
\]

For the core we use Whiteley's kinematic form of the Bolker--Roth
theorem \cite[Theorem~1, Case~1]{whiteleyBipartite}: for a complete
bipartite framework in the plane whose two vertex sets each affinely
span the plane, the dimension of the space of infinitesimal motions
modulo trivial motions equals the dimension of the space of quadratic
polynomials vanishing on all joints. Four generic points of $V_1$ and
three of $V_2$ span the plane, since three collinear generic points
would place a locus inside a line (Lemma~\ref{lem:product}).

\emph{Case 1: the seven core points are not on a conic.} Then the
core is infinitesimally rigid, of rank $2\cdot7-3=11$, so
$\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Rig}(E\setminus T)=19$. This is the
largest possible rank of $\operatorname{Rig}$, which is attained.

\emph{Case 2: all grid points lie on one irreducible conic $\Gamma$.}
Seven distinct points of an irreducible conic impose five independent
conditions on quadratic polynomials, so exactly one conic passes
through the core and its rank is $11-1=10$; thus
$\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Rig}(E\setminus T)=18$. Conversely
$\operatorname{Rig}$ has a nontrivial motion: if $\Gamma$ is the zero
set of $x^{\mathsf T}Sx+2b\cdot x+c$ with $S$ symmetric, then
$H_i=Sp_i+b$, $U_\ell=-Sr_\ell-b$ satisfies
$(p_i-r_\ell)\cdot(H_i-U_\ell)=\Gamma(p_i)-\Gamma(r_\ell)=0$, and it
is not a trivial motion because $S$ is symmetric while $J$ is
antisymmetric. Hence $\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Rig}\le18$ and
equality holds.

Lemma~\ref{lem:conic} shows these two cases are exhaustive.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Conic avoidance]
\label{lem:conic}
Let neither locus be a line. If $V_1$ and $V_2$ are not the same
irreducible conic, the seven core points of the generic grid do not
lie on a conic. If $V_1=V_2=\Gamma$ is an irreducible conic, all
grid points lie on $\Gamma$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The second statement is immediate. For the first, lying on a conic is
the vanishing of all $6\times6$ minors of the $7\times6$ matrix of
quadratic monomials at the seven points, a polynomial condition with
rational coefficients. By Lemma~\ref{lem:product} it suffices to
exhibit one configuration of four points on $V_1$ and three on $V_2$
not on a conic.

If some locus is the plane, choose the points on the other locus
first; each further plane point can be chosen outside the common zero
set of the conics through the points so far, which is a proper
subset of the plane, so the space of conics drops to zero after at
most six points. If $V_1=V_2=V$ is a curve of degree at least three,
no conic contains $V$, so the six quadratic monomials restrict to
independent functions on $V$ and seven points of $V$ with
nonsingular evaluation matrix exist. If $V_1\ne V_2$ are curves,
choose four points of $V_1$ off $V_2$ with no three collinear, which
is possible because $V_1\cap V_2$ and $V_1\cap(\text{line})$ are
finite. They impose four independent conditions, leaving a pencil of
conics whose base locus is exactly these four points. Choose
$q_1\in V_2$ outside the base locus; one conic $\Gamma_1$ of the
pencil passes through it. $\Gamma_1$ does not contain $V_2$, since a
conic containing $V_2$ equals $V_2$ and would pass through the four
chosen points of $V_1$, which lie off $V_2$. So choose
$q_2\in V_2\setminus\Gamma_1$; no conic passes through the six points,
and a seventh point changes nothing.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:thirty}
Conditional on the source's generic-grid and private-valuation
lemmas, a single $6$-by-$5$ generic grid, or thirty selected edges,
suffices for the final algebraic contradiction in every case: by
Proposition~\ref{prop:joint} when neither locus is a line, and by the
$6$-by-$4$ and $4$-by-$3$ sub-grids of
Propositions~\ref{prop:collinear} and Section~\ref{sec:common-line}
otherwise.
\end{theorem}

The counting $Nj\ge3(N+j)-4$ is equivalent to $(N-3)(j-3)\ge5$, so
$6\times5$ and $5\times6$ are the only thirty-edge grids this
argument admits; $7\times4$ gives $28<29$ and $5\times5$ gives $25<26$,
and in both the kernel contains vectors with nonconstant scalar part.

\section{What the method cannot do}
\label{sec:limits}
The reductions above are exhausted at these numbers for the following
reasons.

\begin{remark}[Three unknowns per vertex]
The joint system of Section~\ref{sec:joint} has two velocity
unknowns and one scalar unknown per vertex. On a locus given by a
curve whose coefficients lie in $F$, the Leibniz rule would tie the
two velocity coordinates together at the cost of adjoining the
derivatives of the finitely many coefficients as shared unknowns;
for a line this recovers the collinear count, for a conic it allows
$4\times5$, but for the plane there is no such relation and
$6\times5$ is the floor of this count.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The symmetry group is five-dimensional]
The transformations \eqref{eq:gauge} form a group of dimension five:
two translations, one rotation, the scalar shift
$A_i\mapsto A_i+\beta$, $A'_\ell\mapsto A'_\ell+\beta$, and the
scaling. The shift is already spent on $A'_1=0$. Dilations
$H_i\mapsto H_i+\mu p_i$, $U_\ell\mapsto U_\ell+\mu r_\ell$ change the
left side of \eqref{eq:system} by $2\mu L_{i\ell}$, which is not of
the form $R_{i\ell}(\beta_i-\beta'_\ell)$, so no further continuous
symmetry is available. Hence $3j-5$ is the least generator count
obtainable by normalizing the anchor data alone.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Four anchors are needed in the rank-two case]
Three equations in the three unknowns $H_x,H_y,A$ are solved by
Cramer's rule and leave no equation for the sign change. The count
$N>\text{cost}$ is also sharp for the subadditivity argument.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Choosing the derivation]
The source's derivation may be chosen to vanish on a rational subspace
of the anchor differentials that avoids all $d\lambda_{i\ell}$, which
removes anchor data in favorable cases. This gives no unconditional
saving: all normalized data may be algebraic over $k_0(t)$ for a
single $t$, in which case the relations have coefficients outside the
coefficient field. We therefore do not use it.
\end{remark}

None of this provides an explicit power saving $\delta$ for the
unit-distance bound: the generic-grid extraction and negligible-height
passage remain asymptotic, and no quantitative replacement is
established here.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The accompanying README documents \texttt{python -B run\_checks.py}.
The scripts, source manifest, and test expectations are in this repository.
The rank statements of Section~\ref{sec:joint} are checked there on
exact rational configurations, both generic and on a circle, including
the kernel's constant scalar part; these checks illustrate the cited
rigidity theorem in the cases used and do not replace it.
